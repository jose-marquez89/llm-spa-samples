MODULE 01 · ZERO-JARGON FOUNDATION

The EKG Primer: Translating Electrical Squiggles into Plain English

Before looking at abnormal rhythms, understand what the machine is actually measuring, why each bump exists, and how the grid paper turns distance into time.

🏠 1. Wiring vs. Plumbing

Think of the heart like a house: it has plumbing (chambers, valves, blood pressure) and electrical wiring (pacemaker cells that spark each squeeze). An EKG only measures the electrical wiring—never whether blood is actually pumping!

⚡ 2. Depolarization vs. Repolarization

Textbooks love big words: Depolarization simply means "the electrical spark fires" (causing muscle contraction). Repolarization means "the battery recharges" so the heart can fire again on the next beat.

🩺 3. Why Nurses Always Check the Patient

Because an EKG only shows electricity, a monitor can show a normal-looking rhythm while the patient has zero pulse (called PEA — Pulseless Electrical Activity), or a loose sticker can mimic lethal V-Tach. Rule #1: Treat the patient, not the monitor!

🗣️ In Plain Everyday English

P Wave — "The Top Chambers Spark & Squeeze"

The heart’s natural pacemaker (the SA node) fires a tiny spark across the two top rooms (atria). This gentle bump is the electrical signal telling the top chambers to top off the bottom chambers with blood.

🩺 Nursing & NCLEX Clinical Translation

Atrial Depolarization · Normal: < 0.12s, Smooth & Upright

Ask two questions: Are P waves upright and uniform in Lead II? And is there one P wave married to every QRS? Missing P waves with an irregular rhythm points to Atrial Fibrillation; sawtooth P waves point to Atrial Flutter.

📋 The Nurse’s 5-Step Strip Reading Routine
READ EVERY STRIP IN THIS ORDER
1

Is the Rhythm Regular or Irregular? (March out R-to-R)

Use calipers or paper marks to check if the distance between R-peaks stays equal. Irregularly irregular = think Atrial Fibrillation.

2

What is the Heart Rate? (Normal: 60–100 bpm)

On a 6-second strip (30 large boxes), count the R-waves and multiply by 10. Under 60 = Bradycardia; over 100 = Tachycardia.

3

Are P Waves Present, Upright, and Married to a QRS?

Look before every QRS complex. Is there one smooth, upright P wave for every QRS? Or are there extra unconducted P waves (AV Blocks)?

4

Measure the PR Interval (Normal: 0.12–0.20 sec / 3–5 small boxes)

Measure from the beginning of the P wave to the beginning of the QRS. If > 0.20 sec, the AV node is delaying the impulse (Heart Block).

5

Measure the QRS Width (Normal: < 0.12 sec / < 3 small boxes)

Narrow QRS (< 3 boxes) means the beat came from above the ventricles. Wide QRS (≥ 3 boxes) means it originated in the ventricles (PVC / V-Tach) or a bundle branch block.

LEVEL 1 · BASIC

Normal Sinus Rhythm (NSR)

Plain-English Translation: The heart's natural pacemaker (SA node) is firing smoothly on schedule between 60 and 100 times a minute, and the spark is traveling down the normal highway without any roadblocks.

1. Regularity Regular
2. Rate 60–100 bpm
3. P Waves Upright, 1:1
4. PR Interval 0.12–0.20 s
5. QRS Width < 0.12 s

🩺 Nursing Priority & NCLEX Bedside Action

No intervention required! Document Normal Sinus Rhythm and continue routine monitoring. Always correlate the strip with the patient's blood pressure and mental status.

Common Context: Healthy resting baseline.

🫀 Culprit Coronary Artery & Nursing Priorities
INFERIOR STEMI
ST Elevation: Leads II, III, aVF · Reciprocal Depression: Leads I, aVL

Right Coronary Artery (RCA) Occlusion

Leads II, III, and aVF look upward at the bottom (diaphragmatic/inferior) surface of the heart. In 85% of people, the Right Coronary Artery feeds this wall—along with the SA node and AV node!

High-Yield NCLEX Nursing Alert: Because the RCA supplies the Right Ventricle and AV Node, Inferior STEMIs frequently cause Sinus Bradycardia, AV Heart Blocks, and Right Ventricular Infarction. Hold Nitroglycerin and opioids if hypotensive—RV infarcts depend on preload (give IV fluids instead)!
⏱️ ACUTE STEMI CORE PROTOCOL (DOOR-TO-BALLOON ≤ 90 MIN)

1. Obtain 12-Lead EKG within 10 minutes of chest pain arrival.
2. Chewable Aspirin 324 mg immediately (unless contraindicated), IV access ×2, cardiac troponins.
3. Oxygen only if SpO2 < 90%. Prepare patient for emergent PCI (Cath Lab).
